Pool resurfacing costs $4 to $10 per square foot for plaster, around $10 per square foot for pebble finishes, $5 to $7 for quartz aggregate, and $4 to $30 for tile, depending heavily on pattern and coverage. For a standard 16x32-foot inground pool, that translates to roughly $5,700 to $11,600 total for plaster and about $9,500 for pebble, once wall surface is counted alongside the floor.

The per-square-foot number is only half the decision. Finishes last very different lengths of time, so the cheapest option per square foot isn't always the cheapest option per year of use, and that's the number that actually matters for budgeting a pool you'll own for a decade or more.

## How much does resurfacing cost per square foot by finish?

| Finish | Cost per sq ft | What it looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Paint (epoxy or chlorinated rubber) | $1 to $2 | Budget coating, not true resurfacing |
| Plaster (white or colored) | $4 to $10 | Traditional smooth finish, most common |
| Quartz aggregate | $5 to $7 | Plaster blended with quartz for added durability |
| Pebble | ~$10 | Textured, small stone aggregate |
| Tile | $4 to $30 | Usually an accent or waterline band, full-surface tile is the high end |

Figures reflect 2026 U.S. averages reported across multiple pool-remodeling cost guides ([HomeGuide, pool resurfacing cost, 2026](https://homeguide.com/costs/pool-resurfacing-cost); [Panther Pools, resurfacing cost factors, 2026](https://www.panther-pools.com/pool-resurfacing-cost-factors-2026/)).

## What does resurfacing actually cost for a typical pool, by size?

Per-square-foot rates apply to total wetted surface (floor plus walls), which runs meaningfully larger than a pool's footprint. Using the same wetted-surface ratio pool-service contractors apply for chemical treatments (roughly 1.85x the waterline footprint), here's what that looks like in real dollars:

| Pool size (footprint) | Est. wetted surface | Plaster total | Pebble total | Quartz total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Small, 12x24 ft (~290 sq ft) | ~600 sq ft | $3,600 to $4,800 | ~$6,000 | $3,000 to $4,200 |
| Standard, 16x32 ft (~510 sq ft) | ~950 sq ft | $5,700 to $7,600 | ~$9,500 | $4,750 to $6,650 |
| Large, 20x40 ft (~800 sq ft) | ~1,450 sq ft | $8,700 to $11,600 | ~$14,500 | $7,250 to $10,150 |

These totals are our own calculation, applying the sourced per-square-foot rates above to computed wetted-surface areas; get a walk-through quote before budgeting off any table, ours included, since prep work and repairs change the number fast.

## How long does each finish actually last, and what does that mean per year?

This is the number most cost guides skip: a cheaper finish that needs replacing twice as often isn't actually cheaper.

| Finish | Typical lifespan | Standard-pool total cost | Approx. annualized cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plaster | 7 to 12 years ([Guiton's Pool Center, pool material lifespan](https://guitons.com/the-lifespan-of-a-pool-materials-that-last-the-longest/)) | $5,700 to $7,600 | ~$475 to $1,085/yr |
| Quartz aggregate | 15 to 20 years | $4,750 to $6,650 | ~$238 to $443/yr |
| Pebble | 15 to 25 years | ~$9,500 | ~$380 to $633/yr |

Quartz aggregate comes out as the lowest annualized cost in this comparison for a standard-size pool, despite pebble's longer maximum lifespan, because pebble's higher upfront price offsets most of the extra durability. Plaster remains the most expensive per year of the three specifically because it needs replacing two to three times as often as the other options, even though it has the lowest sticker price at the point of purchase. Paint coatings sit outside this comparison entirely: epoxy paint lasts 7 to 10 years and chlorinated rubber paint lasts only 2 to 3 years per coat ([ArmorGarage, epoxy pool paint lifespan](https://armorgarage.com/ultra-2-part-epoxy-pool-paint/)), so paint's low per-square-foot price gets erased quickly by recoat frequency.

## What about vinyl liner and fiberglass pools, do they get "resurfaced"?

Not in the same sense. Vinyl liner pools don't have a plaster or pebble surface to resurface; instead, the entire liner gets replaced, typically running $1,000 to $3,500 per 1,000 square feet of liner, with most liners lasting 7 to 15 years depending on sun exposure and water balance ([HomeGuide, pool resurfacing cost, 2026](https://homeguide.com/costs/pool-resurfacing-cost)). Fiberglass pools have a gelcoat rather than a poured surface; a full gelcoat refinish averages about $6,500 per 1,000 square feet and is typically needed every 20 to 25 years, well beyond the 25-to-30-year lifespan of the fiberglass shell itself.

## What drives the price up or down within a finish type?

Prep work is the biggest swing factor. If the old surface has structural cracks, delamination, or plumbing that needs to be accessed and repaired before the new surface goes on, that's added labor and material beyond the baseline per-square-foot rate, sometimes a significant addition. Color and texture upgrades within pebble and quartz finishes (glass bead blends, darker pigments that show less scaling but fade faster) push toward the top of each range. Draining and refilling, usually $225 to $900 for a professional drain and refill or as little as $2.50 to $9 per 1,000 gallons in municipal water if filling yourself, is sometimes bundled into a resurfacing quote and sometimes billed separately; confirm which before comparing bids.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long does a resurfacing project actually take?**
Most projects run 5 to 10 calendar days start to finish: about a day to drain, 3 to 5 days for surface prep and application, 2 to 4 days curing, then refill and water-balance testing before swimming ([D&J Pool Resurfacers, resurfacing timeline, 2026](https://dandjthepoolresurfacers.com/en/2026/05/04/how-long-does-pool-resurfacing-take/)).

**Can I switch finish types when resurfacing, like plaster to pebble?**
Yes, this is one of the most common reasons owners resurface rather than just repair; switching finish types during a scheduled resurfacing doesn't add significant extra labor since the old surface is being removed either way, though the material cost difference between finishes still applies.

**How often should a pool actually be resurfaced?**
On the finish's typical lifespan schedule (7 to 12 years for plaster, 15 to 25 for pebble, 15 to 20 for quartz), or sooner if you're seeing rough texture, visible staining that won't lift with an acid wash, or exposed aggregate/rebar, all signs the surface has worn past a simple chemical fix.

**Does a cheaper finish always mean a worse deal?**
Not automatically, but it depends on your ownership horizon. A plaster resurfacing costs less upfront and may make sense if you're planning to sell within the next several years; an owner planning to stay 15-plus years usually comes out ahead financially with pebble or quartz despite the higher initial bill.

Bottom line: budget by wetted surface area, not footprint, expect $4 to $10 per square foot for plaster and roughly $10 for pebble, and weigh lifespan against sticker price before assuming the cheapest finish is the cheapest choice.
