Replacing an above-ground pool liner costs $500 to $2,500 installed. An inground vinyl liner runs $3,000 to $5,500 for a standard rectangular pool, or $6,500 to $7,500-plus for a custom shape once draining, disposal, and any floor repair are included. A common 2026 rule of thumb is about $5 per square foot of total floor and wall area for a full inground job.

Thickness (gauge) and pool shape move the price more than almost anything else, and both are worth understanding before getting quotes.

## What actually drives the total cost?

A full inground liner replacement typically breaks down into three pieces: the liner material itself (commonly around $1,700 for a standard pool), water to refill (around $1,200 depending on local water cost and truck-delivery needs), and labor (around $2,000), landing most jobs in the commonly cited $4,500 to $6,500 range once all three are added together ([HomeGuide's pool liner cost guide](https://homeguide.com/costs/pool-liner-cost)). Custom shapes, an attached spa, a tanning ledge, a vanishing edge, cost more because they need more precisely cut material and more labor time to fit.

## Does liner thickness (gauge) actually matter for the price?

Yes, and it also changes how long the liner lasts. 20-mil is the standard entry-level thickness most pools ship with. Stepping up to 27-mil typically adds $100 to $200 in material cost; stepping up to 30-mil adds $700 to $1,000 ([Beatbot's 2026 pool liner cost breakdown](https://beatbot.com/blogs/pool-care-solutions/pool-liner-replacement-cost)).

| Gauge | Cost vs. 20-mil baseline | Typical lifespan |
|---|---|---|
| 20-mil (standard) | Baseline | 7 to 10 years |
| 27-mil | +$100 to $200 | 8 to 12 years |
| 30-mil | +$700 to $1,000 | 12 to 20 years |

The math favors upgrading on a pool you plan to keep for a decade or more: a 30-mil liner can cost roughly the same per year of service as a 20-mil liner replaced twice, without paying for a second full labor job in between.

## How much does an above-ground liner cost versus inground?

| Pool type | Liner cost range | Typical timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Above-ground vinyl | $500 to $2,500 | Same day to a few days |
| Inground, standard rectangular | $3,000 to $5,500 | 2 to 3 weeks |
| Inground, custom shape (freeform, vanishing edge, attached spa) | $6,500 to $7,500-plus | 2 to 3-plus weeks |

Above-ground liners are cheaper for two reasons beyond size: they're simpler shapes that need less custom cutting, and the job doesn't require draining and refilling a full inground shell with the same labor and water-delivery logistics.

## What actually signals it's time to replace, not patch?

- Fading or noticeable discoloration across large sections, not just at the waterline
- Tears or wrinkles that keep reappearing after patching
- The liner slipping or sagging out of the coping track
- Water level dropping more than about an inch over one to two weeks, once evaporation is ruled out as the cause
- Repeated leaks in different spots rather than one isolated puncture

(Consolidated from [Latham Pool's vinyl liner lifecycle guide](https://www.lathampool.com/blog/maintain/the-typical-life-cycle-of-the-vinyl-liner-in-your-swimming-pool/) and [Tara Pools' liner replacement signs](https://tarapools.com/blog/signs-to-look-for-and-when-to-replace-your-vinyl-pool-liner/).) A single small tear is usually a $10 to $500 patch job, not a full replacement; multiple recurring leaks across an aging liner is when replacement becomes the more cost-effective call.

## What's the realistic lifespan of a vinyl liner?

Most vinyl liners last 7 to 10 years industry-wide, though the range runs 5 to 9 years for inground liners and 6 to 10 for above-ground, with well-maintained, thicker-gauge liners reaching 15 years or more ([Angi's liner lifespan guide](https://www.angi.com/articles/when-do-i-need-replace-my-pool-liner.htm)). Water chemistry is the biggest lever an owner actually controls here: chronically low pH dries and cracks vinyl faster, and unbalanced sanitizer levels fade patterns years ahead of schedule.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much does it cost to replace a pool liner?**
Above-ground liners run $500 to $2,500. Inground liners run $3,000 to $5,500 for a standard rectangular shape, or $6,500 to $7,500-plus for a custom shape, once material, water refill, and labor are all included.

**How long does a pool liner last?**
Most vinyl liners last 7 to 10 years. Thinner 20-mil liners tend toward the shorter end; thicker 27 to 30-mil liners can reach 12 to 20 years with good water chemistry and care.

**Is it worth paying more for a thicker liner?**
Often, yes, if the pool is a long-term keeper. A 30-mil liner costs $700 to $1,000 more upfront than 20-mil but can last twice as long, which usually beats paying for a second full replacement labor job within the same span of years.

**Can I patch a liner instead of replacing it?**
Yes, for a single small tear or puncture, a patch running $10 to $500 is the right call. Multiple recurring leaks, major fading, or a liner slipping out of its track are signs the liner itself is past patching and full replacement is the more cost-effective option.

Bottom line: budget $3,000 to $5,500 for a standard inground liner or $500 to $2,500 above-ground, and consider the thicker gauge if the pool is staying put for the long haul.
