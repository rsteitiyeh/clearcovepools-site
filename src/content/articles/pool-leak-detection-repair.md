A pool losing more than about a quarter inch of water a day, after accounting for evaporation, likely has a leak. The bucket test settles it in 24 hours: compare water loss inside a bucket sitting in the pool against the pool's actual water loss. Professional leak detection costs $100 to $1,000, typically $300 to $600, and common repairs run from a $10 DIY liner patch to $2,500 for underground plumbing work.

Most owners jump straight to calling a leak detection company. The bucket test costs nothing and rules evaporation in or out first, which matters because evaporation alone can lose close to half an inch a day in hot, windy, low-humidity conditions and get mistaken for a leak.

## How do you tell a leak from normal evaporation?

Normal evaporation for a typical inground pool runs about a quarter to a half inch a day, roughly 1.5 to 3.5 inches a week, depending on climate, wind, and pool heating ([CareFree Pool Club, evaporation vs. leak](https://www.carefreepoolclub.com/2026/06/25/pool-losing-water-is-it-evaporation-or-a-leak/)). A straight quarter inch a day is most likely just evaporation. Losses over an inch a day almost always mean an actual leak, not weather.

## How does the bucket test actually work?

1. Fill a 5-gallon bucket about two-thirds full with pool water and set it on the first or second step, so it's sitting in the pool water at least 5 inches deep.
2. Mark the water level inside the bucket, and mark the pool's waterline separately (the skimmer opening is an easy reference point).
3. Turn off any automatic fill system so it can't mask a leak.
4. Wait 24 hours, then compare the two marks.
5. If the pool's level dropped more than the bucket's level, water is leaving somewhere the bucket isn't, which points to an actual leak rather than evaporation ([Leak-Tech's bucket test method](https://leak-tech.com/the-bucket-test/); [Pinnacle Pool & Spa's at-home diagnostics](https://pinnaclepoolspa.com/at-home-diagnostics/)).

## leak or evaporation, quick reference

| Daily water loss | Likely cause | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Up to 1/4 inch | Normal evaporation | Keep monitoring, no action needed |
| 1/4 to 1/2 inch | Could be evaporation in hot, dry, or windy conditions | Run the bucket test to confirm |
| Over 1/2 inch, especially over 1 inch | Likely a leak | Run the bucket test, then call a pro if confirmed |

## What does professional leak detection cost?

Professional leak detection runs $100 to $1,000 for a typical residential job, with most landing in the $300 to $600 range. Advanced diagnostics, pressure testing, dye testing, or electronic tracing, add $100 to $600 on top of a basic inspection, and complex jobs needing extensive access can reach $1,500 to $3,000 ([Angi's leak detection cost data](https://www.angi.com/articles/pool-leak-detection-cost.htm); [HomeGuide, 2026](https://homeguide.com/costs/pool-leak-detection-cost)). The fee is almost entirely labor, and regional labor rates shift the number more than the diagnostic method itself does.

General labor for pool repair work runs $45 to $65 an hour, which is most of what separates a $50 skimmer fix from a $2,500 plumbing dig.

## How does a professional actually find a leak?

Beyond the homeowner bucket test, pros use a dye test, releasing colored dye near suspected cracks or fittings underwater and watching whether it gets pulled into a crack, and electronic leak detection, listening equipment that picks up the sound of water escaping through plumbing. Dye testing works well for visible cracks in the shell, liner, or skimmer; electronic detection is what finds a leak in buried plumbing that isn't visible at all.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much water loss per day means I have a leak?**
Up to about a quarter inch a day is typically just evaporation. Losses over half an inch, and especially over a full inch, point to an actual leak. The bucket test confirms it in 24 hours.

**How much does it cost to find a pool leak?**
Professional leak detection runs $100 to $1,000, most commonly $300 to $600. Advanced methods like dye testing or electronic tracing add $100 to $600 on top of a basic inspection.

**Can I fix a small pool leak myself?**
A small vinyl liner tear can be patched for as little as $10 with a DIY kit. Skimmer cracks and pump seals are also within reach for a handy owner. Underground plumbing leaks and main drain repairs are best left to a professional given the excavation and specialized testing involved.

**Does a pool losing water always mean a leak?**
No. Evaporation alone can account for up to about half an inch of daily loss in hot, dry, or windy conditions, more with an uncovered heated pool. The bucket test is the fastest way to separate evaporation from an actual leak before spending on a professional inspection.

Bottom line: run the bucket test before calling anyone. It costs nothing, takes 24 hours, and tells you whether you're chasing evaporation or an actual leak worth paying to find.
