A spring pool opening checklist runs in three phases, done in this order: physically uncovering and prepping the equipment (drain plugs back in, o-rings checked, water topped off to normal level), testing five chemistry numbers (free chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, and cyanuric acid), then balancing chemicals in sequence, alkalinity first, pH second, calcium third, stabilizer fourth, sanitizer fifth, shock last. Most openings take a full day of hands-on work, plus 24 to 48 hours before the water is stable enough to swim in.

Skipping the order costs time, not just money. Adjusting pH before alkalinity is in range, for instance, means the pH reading drifts back within a day, and homeowners who don't know why end up buying more chemical than the pool actually needed.

## What's the physical opening sequence, before any chemicals go in?

1. **Remove the winter cover.** Pump off standing water first so debris and dirty runoff don't wash into the pool as you pull it back.
2. **Clean and inspect the cover** before folding it away for storage; check for tears or UV degradation that need patching before next winter.
3. **Reinstall drain plugs** on the pump, filter, heater, and chlorinator that were pulled for winterization, and check every o-ring and gasket for cracking, replacing any that look dry or brittle.
4. **Lubricate the pump lid o-ring** with a silicone-based lubricant so it seals properly on startup.
5. **Bring the water level up** to its normal operating point, typically midway up the skimmer opening, using a hose.
6. **Prime and start the pump**, watching for leaks at every connection you just touched, then run the filter and check that it's cycling normally before moving to water testing.

## What five numbers do you test, and what's the target?

| Test | Target range | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Free chlorine | At least 1 ppm (2 ppm if using cyanuric acid) | [CDC Healthy Swimming](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/about/home-pool-and-hot-tub-water-treatment-and-testing.html) |
| pH | 7.0 to 7.8 | [CDC Healthy Swimming](https://www.cdc.gov/healthy-swimming/about/home-pool-and-hot-tub-water-treatment-and-testing.html) |
| Total alkalinity | 80 to 120 ppm | Standard industry range, buffers pH swings |
| Calcium hardness | 200 to 400 ppm | Standard industry range, protects plaster and metal fittings |
| Cyanuric acid (outdoor pools) | 30 to 50 ppm | Protects chlorine from UV breakdown |

Test before adding anything. A pool that overwintered well under a tight cover often needs very little correction; a pool that sat exposed all winter can be off on every number at once.

## What's the correct order to add opening chemicals?

Once you know which numbers are out of range, correct them in this order and retest between steps, waiting 15 to 30 minutes with the pump running before moving to the next chemical:

1. **Total alkalinity**, using sodium bicarbonate to raise it or muriatic acid to lower it. This has to go first because alkalinity buffers everything that follows.
2. **pH**, using soda ash to raise or muriatic acid to lower, only once alkalinity is in range.
3. **Calcium hardness**, using calcium chloride if low, common after a winter of dilution from rain and snowmelt.
4. **Cyanuric acid**, if the outdoor pool needs stabilizer; this takes 48 to 72 hours to fully dissolve, so dosing it early gives it time to take effect before real UV exposure starts.
5. **Sanitizer**, bringing chlorine up to a working residual per the CDC targets above.
6. **Shock, last.** Use 1 lb of calcium hypochlorite per 10,000 gallons for a standard opening with clear water, or 2 lb per 10,000 gallons if the water has any green tint, then let the filter run continuously until the reading holds.

## the printable opening checklist

- [ ] Pump water off the cover, then remove and clean it
- [ ] Reinstall drain plugs on pump, filter, heater, chlorinator
- [ ] Check and lubricate o-rings and gaskets
- [ ] Refill to normal water level (mid-skimmer)
- [ ] Prime pump, start filter, check for leaks
- [ ] Test free chlorine, pH, total alkalinity, calcium hardness, CYA
- [ ] Adjust total alkalinity (80 to 120 ppm)
- [ ] Adjust pH (7.0 to 7.8)
- [ ] Adjust calcium hardness (200 to 400 ppm)
- [ ] Add stabilizer if needed (30 to 50 ppm outdoor)
- [ ] Bring sanitizer to a working residual
- [ ] Shock last, then filter continuously until clear

## How long until the pool is actually swimmable?

Once free chlorine sits in the CDC-recommended range and pH holds between 7.0 and 7.8, the water is safe. After a standard opening shock, that's typically 24 to 48 hours, the time it takes the elevated post-shock chlorine reading to drop back into normal swimming range. A pool that opened green and needed a heavier breakpoint treatment takes longer, sometimes several days of repeated dosing before the water clears and chemistry stabilizes.

## Frequently asked questions

**When should I actually open my pool in spring?**
Once overnight temperatures consistently stay above roughly 60°F, algae growth slows enough that an early opening won't immediately bloom again; opening too early in cold water is fine chemically, it just means running the pump and testing more before swim season actually starts.

**Do I really need to test before adding any chemicals?**
Yes. Adding chemicals blind risks overcorrecting a number that was already close to target, and a pool that wintered under a good cover often needs far less than a full six-chemical sequence.

**How much shock do I need to open a pool that isn't green?**
1 lb of calcium hypochlorite per 10,000 gallons for a standard, clear-water opening; double that to 2 lb per 10,000 gallons only if the water shows a green tint.

**Can I open a pool myself, or do I need a professional?**
Most owners can handle a standard opening with a good test kit and the sequence above. A professional is worth calling if the equipment shows damage from freezing, the water is severely green or swampy, or you're opening a pool for the first time and want someone to confirm the plumbing and equipment are sound before you commit to a full chemical startup.

Bottom line: prep the equipment before you touch a single chemical, test all five numbers first, and correct them in order, alkalinity, pH, calcium, stabilizer, sanitizer, shock, with a retest between each.
