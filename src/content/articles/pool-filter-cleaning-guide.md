Clean a pool filter whenever the pressure gauge reads 8 to 10 psi above its clean baseline, no matter the filter type. On a routine schedule, that works out to backwashing a sand filter every 1 to 2 months, rinsing a cartridge filter every 2 to 6 weeks in peak season, and backwashing a DE filter monthly. Pressure, not the calendar, is the real trigger.

Write down the pressure reading right after cleaning; that number is the baseline everything else gets measured against for the rest of the season.

## How do you know when a filter actually needs cleaning?

Check the pressure gauge on the filter tank and compare it to the clean baseline recorded after the last cleaning. A rise of 8 to 10 psi above that baseline means the filter is loaded with enough debris to restrict flow, and it's time to clean regardless of how many weeks it's been ([SwimUniversity's pressure gauge guide](https://www.swimuniversity.com/pool-filter-pressure-gauge/); [ProTuff Products](https://www.protuffproducts.com/equipment-use/pool-filter-pressure-too-high/)). Cleaning too early wastes time and, for a DE filter, media. Waiting too long past the 8 to 10 psi mark strains the pump and can push debris back into the pool.

## What's the actual cleaning schedule by filter type?

| Filter type | Clean trigger | Routine interval | Normal operating pressure |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sand | +8 to 10 psi above baseline | Backwash every 1 to 2 months | 10 to 15 psi |
| Cartridge | +8 to 10 psi above baseline | Rinse every 2 to 6 weeks (peak season) | 8 to 10 psi |
| DE (diatomaceous earth) | +8 to 10 psi above baseline | Backwash monthly | 10 to 20 psi after backwash |

## How do you backwash a sand filter?

1. Turn off the pump.
2. Set the multiport valve to "Backwash."
3. Restart the pump and run it 2 to 3 minutes, until the sight glass runs clear.
4. Turn the pump off again.
5. Set the valve to "Rinse" and run 1 to 2 minutes to resettle the sand bed and flush out any leftover particles.
6. Return the valve to "Filter" and restart normal operation.

(Steps consolidated from [Royal Swimming Pools' backwash guide](https://blog.royalswimmingpools.com/how-to-backwash-pool-filter-guide) and [In The Swim's backwashing best practices](https://www.intheswim.com/blog/backwashing-best-practices.html).)

## How do you clean a cartridge filter?

Cartridge filters don't backwash. Instead:

1. Turn off the pump and relieve system pressure before opening the filter housing.
2. Remove the cartridge and hose it down between the pleats, working top to bottom.
3. For a deeper clean, soak the cartridge in a cartridge cleaning solution for 6 hours to overnight.
4. Rinse thoroughly and let it air-dry completely before reinstalling; a wet cartridge traps debris faster once it's back in the housing.

## How do you clean a DE filter?

1. Backwash for 3 to 5 minutes, the same way as a sand filter.
2. Shut off the pump and return the valve to filter mode.
3. Open the air relief valve and remove the drain plug to fully drain the tank.
4. Recharge the filter with fresh DE powder through the skimmer while the pump runs, roughly 1 to 2 lb of DE per 10 sq ft of filter area.

## When does the filter media itself need replacing, not just cleaning?

- **Sand:** every 5 to 7 years, since grains gradually round off and lose the sharp edges that trap debris, even though the sand doesn't look visibly worn.
- **Cartridge elements:** every 1 to 3 seasons, roughly 2 to 5 years, once cleaning stops restoring flow or the pleats show visible damage.
- **DE grids:** every 5 to 7 years, up to 8 to 10 years with excellent maintenance, replaced sooner if a grid tears and starts passing DE powder back into the pool.

(Intervals per [Benchmark Pool Supply's filter lifespan guide](https://benchmarkpoolsupply.com/blogs/knowledge-hub/the-average-lifespan-of-pool-filters-explained) and [All Filters' DE grid replacement guide](https://www.allfilters.com/blog/de-pool-filter-grids-replacement-maintenance).)

Balanced water chemistry stretches every filter's cleaning interval, since scale from high calcium or pH doesn't build up as fast on media that's working with correctly balanced water instead of against it. ## Frequently asked questions

**How often should I backwash my sand filter?**
Every 1 to 2 months on a routine basis, but the real trigger is pressure: backwash whenever the gauge reads 8 to 10 psi above its clean baseline, even if that happens sooner than the calendar interval.

**How often should I clean a cartridge filter?**
Every 2 to 6 weeks during peak swim season, or sooner if the pressure gauge rises 8 to 10 psi above baseline. A deeper soak-clean in cartridge cleaning solution once or twice a season keeps the pleats working at full capacity.

**Can I clean a pool filter too often?**
Cleaning a sand or DE filter more than necessary doesn't damage anything, but it does waste DE powder and disrupt the sand bed's normal settling. It's more efficient to clean by pressure reading than to over-clean on a fixed schedule.

**How do I know if my filter needs new media instead of just a cleaning?**
If backwashing or rinsing stops bringing the pressure back down to baseline, the media itself, sand, cartridge, or DE grids, has likely reached the end of its working life and needs replacing rather than another cleaning cycle.

Bottom line: clean by pressure, not by the calendar, backwash or rinse whenever the gauge reads 8 to 10 psi above its clean baseline, and replace the media itself every few years once cleaning stops bringing the pressure back down.
