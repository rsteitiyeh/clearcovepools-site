A pool skimmer that's not working is almost always one of four things: the water level is wrong (too high or too low), the basket or pump strainer is clogged, the weir door is stuck or dislodged, or there's an air leak reducing suction. All four are diagnosable in a few minutes without calling a technician, and three of the four fix with no tools at all.

Before assuming a bigger problem, check water level first. It's the single most common cause and the easiest to rule out, and skipping it leads a lot of pool owners to replace parts that were never actually broken.

## What's causing your specific skimmer problem?

| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Debris floats past the skimmer, never gets pulled in | Water level too high | Drain to halfway up the skimmer opening |
| Skimmer sucks air, gurgling noise, weak suction | Water level too low | Refill to halfway up the skimmer opening |
| Pump louder than normal, low pressure, basket looks empty | Clogged basket or pump strainer | Clean both; check for a crack diverting flow |
| Debris gets pulled in, then pushed back out | Weir door stuck or dislodged | Reset the float medium or clear stuck debris; no tools needed |
| Reduced suction even with a clean basket | Air leak at the pump lid O-ring | Inspect, lubricate, or replace the O-ring |

Diagnostic categories adapted from established pool-maintenance troubleshooting guidance ([Pool Research, pool skimmer troubleshooting](https://poolresearch.com/skimmer-troubleshooting/)).

## Why does water level matter this much?

The ideal pool water level sits halfway up the skimmer opening. Too high, and debris simply floats over the weir door without getting captured, since the opening the water needs to pass through to reach the basket is partly submerged past its working point. Too low, and the skimmer starts drawing air instead of water, which causes a suction leak that can, over time, burn out the pump motor and put the whole filtration system at risk.

Evaporation is the most common reason water level drops on its own, and it happens faster than most owners expect on a hot, dry, or windy stretch. A solar cover can cut evaporation by as much as 95% when used consistently, which keeps the water level (and therefore the skimmer) working correctly with far less manual refilling ([Pool Research, pool skimmer troubleshooting](https://poolresearch.com/skimmer-troubleshooting/)).

## How do you fix a clogged skimmer?

Turn off the pump, remove the pump lid and strainer basket, and clear out any trapped debris. If water still isn't reaching the skimmer fast enough afterward, isolate the skimmer line and run a garden hose backward through it from the pump side to dislodge whatever's stuck further down the pipe. A skimmer sock, a fine mesh liner that sits inside the basket, cuts down on how often full-clog cleanouts are needed, especially during heavy leaf season.

An empty-looking basket doesn't always mean the basket isn't the problem. Even a small crack in a skimmer basket can throw off flow enough to disrupt suction throughout the entire system, so check the basket itself for damage, not just for debris.

## What's actually happening when the weir door fails?

The weir door is the small, hinged flap that lets water and debris in but stops them from floating back out. It fails two ways: the internal float that keeps it buoyant gets dislodged, or a twig or other debris gets physically stuck in the hinge. Both are a push-it-back-into-place fix with no tools required. If the weir door is missing entirely or visibly cracked, it's an inexpensive replacement part, and running a skimmer without one defeats the purpose of having it.

## When is it an air leak instead of a clog?

If the basket and strainer are clean but suction is still weak, check the pump lid O-ring. A dry, cracked, or poorly seated O-ring lets air into the system, which shows up as bubbles at the return jets and a pump that struggles to hold prime. Pull the O-ring, inspect it for tears, lubricate it with pool-safe silicone lubricant, and reseat it; replace it if it's visibly cracked or won't seal.

## When does a skimmer need full replacement instead of a repair?

Minor cracks in the skimmer housing patch with pool putty. A major crack through the housing, one that keeps reopening after patching or that's letting a visible amount of water escape around the equipment pad, means it's time to replace the unit rather than keep patching it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is my pool skimmer not pulling in debris?**
The most common reasons are water level too high (debris floats past the weir instead of going in) or too low (the skimmer draws air instead of water). Check water level first, at roughly the halfway point of the skimmer opening, before troubleshooting anything else.

**What water level should a pool skimmer be at?**
Halfway up the skimmer opening. Below that, the skimmer risks drawing air and losing prime; above that, debris passes over the weir door without being captured.

**Why does my skimmer basket keep overflowing with air bubbles?**
Bubbles at the return jets usually point to an air leak, most often a dry or cracked O-ring at the pump lid, rather than the skimmer basket itself. Check the O-ring before assuming the basket needs replacing.

**Can I fix a cracked skimmer myself?**
Minor cracks in the housing patch with pool putty as a DIY fix. A major crack through the housing, or one that keeps reopening, generally needs full skimmer replacement, which is a bigger job involving the surrounding deck or liner.

**How often should I clean my pool skimmer basket?**
Check it at least weekly during swim season, more often during heavy leaf drop or after a storm. A basket left too full restricts flow well before it looks completely blocked.

Bottom line: check water level first, since it's the cause behind most "skimmer not working" complaints and the easiest to rule out. Work through the clog, weir door, and air leak checks in that order before assuming the skimmer itself needs replacing.
